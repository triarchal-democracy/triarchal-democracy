# Counsel outreach — the note to send

*Short by design. The aim is to buy one hour, not to explain the project.*

---

## The email

> **Subject:** One-hour consult — CNCA structure question, federal NFP, not yet incorporated
>
> Hello —
>
> I am setting up a federal not-for-profit that will make recurring payments to the people who use
> its software, and I have a structural question I would rather answer before incorporating than
> after.
>
> The short version: the Act requires members to elect the directors, and restricts distributing the
> corporation's property to those members. I want both — an accountability chain from users to the
> board, and payments to users. My proposed way out is to keep users outside the membership
> entirely, and make the membership a small class of people they elect, who take no payment while
> serving.
>
> I would like an hour to find out whether that holds. I have a written brief with the structure and
> fifteen questions, ranked, and two of them are the ones that could force a redesign. I am happy to
> pay for an hour on those two alone and come back for the rest.
>
> No charitable registration is being sought. Nothing is being paid to anyone yet — there is a live
> gate in the software that prevents it — so there is no urgency beyond my own, and no exposure
> accruing while we talk.
>
> Can I send the brief?
>
> [Name]
> The Triarc (proposed) — [site]

---

## Who to approach

Ask for someone who does **CNCA not-for-profit work specifically**, not general corporate. The
question is about the Act's membership and distribution provisions, which is a narrow specialism.

Reasonable routes, in order of likely value:

1. **A lawyer who has set up member-funded or benefit-paying non-profits.** Closest to the actual
   problem.
2. **A law society referral service.** In most provinces the first consult is fixed-price and short,
   which matches what you want.
3. **A pro-bono or low-bono clinic for non-profits and social enterprises.** Several exist in Canada;
   worth one search before paying full rate.
4. **A co-operatives specialist**, if the first conversation suggests the NFP shell cannot carry a
   payment to participants. Q11 already contemplates this.

## What to have ready before the call

- `Legal_Review_Brief.md`, sent in advance, not read out on the call.
- A one-line answer to "what does the organisation actually do", because it will be asked and the
  honest answer — *it tests a governance model by running its own governance on it* — sounds evasive
  until it is finished with *and it publishes what breaks*.
- The number you are willing to spend, decided before the call rather than during it.

## What to write down during the call

Only three things:

1. **Does the two-layer separation hold — yes, no, or with changes?**
2. **If with changes, which document has to change**: the articles, the bylaw, or the founding
   documents themselves.
3. **What it costs to incorporate from here**, and how long.

Everything else can be a follow-up email.

## What not to do on the call

Do not agree to anything that changes the founding documents on the spot. The documents are governed
by their own amendment procedure, and any change counsel wants should come back here as a proposal
with the reasoning attached — the same standard every other amendment has met.
