# The Government Adoption Compact

*A reference draft, offered to the community as a proposal — not enacted founding law. This compact describes how Triarchal Democracy reaches real governments — municipal, provincial or state, and national — without ever seizing authority it was not given. If ratified, amendable by ordinary proposal.*

---

## 1. What this compact commits to, and what it does not

**1.1** The Triarc has no taxing power, at any stage, and none is sought under this compact. Nothing here grants the Triarc, Triarchal Framework, or any Member authority to compel payment from anyone. Advocating for a measure a legislature may enact is not the exercise of taxing power: a deployment may argue from its own published evidence for any such measure, and may never levy, administer, or determine eligibility for one. What can change, over time and only by explicit, revocable grant, is who decides how money a government has already lawfully collected gets spent.

**1.2** Property rights, employment, and market pricing are outside the scope of this compact entirely, at every stage. No deployment regulates employment, sets a wage, or fixes a price. This clause governs what a deployment may decide, not what the measures it argues for may affect: arguing for a measure that would affect labour supply, wages or prices is not governing employment, wages or prices.

## 2. The staged path

**2.1** Adoption proceeds in stages, and no stage requires seizing what was not offered:

- **Stage 0 — Network.** The Triarc exists as an opt-in community with no government recognition. Nothing here requires any government's permission.
- **Stage 1 — Government shadow deployment.** Residents of a jurisdiction opt in and the platform mirrors that government's real decisions without binding force, building a public evidentiary record.
- **Stage 2 — Formal advisory recognition.** A government formally receives the Triarc's output as input to its own process, without ceding any authority.
- **Stage 3 — Delegated authority over specific decisions (the Delegated-Authority Mechanism).** A government formally delegates binding authority over a bounded domain — a grant programme, a single budget line — to the Triarc's process.
- **Stage 4 — Expanded formal authority, up to the discretionary portion of the general fund (the Delegated-Authority Mechanism).** Scope expands only after Stage 3 has generated its own evidence.
- **Stage 5 — Federation.** Once more than one deployment exists, the Federation Compact governs how they recognise one another. No deployment holds authority over another.

**2.2** From Stage 2 onward, two distinct paths are available, and neither requires the other.

**2.3** Every stage applies identically regardless of tier — **municipal**, **provincial or state**, or **national** — using whichever of that tier's own legislative, executive, or referendum processes are lawful for it to use. Scope, not tier, determines what a given adoption can reach: a municipal adoption reaches municipal authority, a provincial adoption provincial authority, a national adoption national authority, and none may reach into another's.

## 3. The Default-Delegate Mechanism

**3.1** As soon as the platform is live and populated in a jurisdiction, a sitting elected official may appear as the default Triarc delegate for their own seat. This requires no resolution by the relevant governing body, no charter change, and no formal delegation event.

**3.2** Nothing about an official's legal seat changes under this mechanism. That government's own election law continues to govern how they hold office and how they may be removed. What this mechanism adds is visibility: residents can see, and continuously redirect, how much delegated support that official retains.

**3.3** "Delegate" throughout this section refers only to ordinary delegation under the Founding Charter's governance article. It never confers, implies, or shortcuts the formal, compensated Delegate recognition defined in the Charter's standing article, which remains reserved for a Civic Steward who has earned sector standing and been installed by ratified Position decision. An official recognised under this mechanism holds nothing beyond what any Member receiving delegated votes already holds.

**3.4** An official's initial shown support equals their actual result in the election that seated them — a real, independently verified figure, clearly labelled as election-derived. It establishes initial platform recognition only, and never overrides an active Member's own delegation choice. It moves only as Members act: a Member who joins and redirects delegation elsewhere subtracts from it, and non-participation is neutral.

**3.5** Election-seeded support under 3.4 follows the Charter's own delegation-lapse cycle: it expires after the same period ordinary delegation does (default: annual, per Charter 3.3) unless a Member takes an affirmative act to extend it. Before expiry, any Member may reaffirm or revoke their portion at any time. Each subsequent election resets the recognition to that election's own result, labelled to the election that produced it.

**3.6** A seat is always visible for delegation. An official appears as an active, responsive delegate — able to post reasoning and engage in deliberation — only once they have explicitly opted in. Before that, the seat is shown honestly as unclaimed.

**3.7** A successor officeholder inherits none of a predecessor's shown support. Where a governing body has multiple seats, each seat carries its own default delegate, and nothing here creates a single combined figure for a multi-member body.

**3.8** Before this mechanism operates with any real official's name attached in a specific jurisdiction, identity verification sufficient to prevent duplicate accounts is required for the Members whose accounts could move that official's shown number, alongside continuous monitoring for anomalous signup or delegation-shift patterns.

**3.9** Whether a public, continuously updated confidence signal tied to a sitting official is regulated activity under election or campaign-finance law varies by jurisdiction. This compact does not resolve that question, and no deployment of this mechanism should proceed in a specific jurisdiction without that jurisdiction's own legal review.

## 4. The Delegated-Authority Mechanism

**4.1** A government may formally delegate binding budget authority to the Triarc's process, beginning with a bounded domain and expanding only on its own evidence. That authority is scoped, at every stage, to the discretionary portion of a budget — the portion not already committed to debt service, pension obligations, other legally mandated spending, or existing multi-year contractual commitments. Full general-fund authority, if ever reached, is reached by expanding this discretionary scope.

**4.2** A Triarc decision under a delegation requires ratification by the delegating government's elected governing body before taking legal effect, as the default for every new delegation. A government may move to direct-binding authority, with no ratification step, only after a defined track record of ratified cycles, and only by formally chartering the Triarc's process for that jurisdiction as a recognised public body.

**4.3** Existing government finance staff continue to execute what is decided — payments, compliance, accounting, and legally required reporting. Triarchal Framework provides a transparency and audit layer over that execution and does not replace or duplicate a government's finance function.

**4.4** Essential, life-safety services and legally mandated spending sit outside the discretionary vote entirely, protected regardless of any cycle's outcome. Within the discretionary portion, allocation carries an explicit equity-weighting rule favouring under-resourced neighbourhoods and populations in scoring.

**4.5** A first deployment targets participation at the low end of documented precedent for participatory budgeting, comparable to programmes allocating a low single-digit share of a relevant budget. A higher share — up to approximately one-fifth of a total municipal budget, the highest documented figure for a sustained participatory-budgeting programme, reached only after roughly a decade in the one case where it has occurred — is a long-term aspiration to be earned through Stage 3–4 evidence. These figures are defaults, adjustable by ordinary proposal as evidence accumulates.

**4.6** Treasury and Return reporting distinguish, visibly and by default, between voluntarily donated or granted funds and funds under delegated public-budget authority. Neither is presented blended with the other.

**4.7** Every deployment agreement states explicitly, before it takes effect: exactly what authority is delegated; what information the delegating government must provide, and on what cadence; which resulting decisions are binding and which are advisory; the maximum circumstances under which the government may override a binding decision; a requirement that every override carry public reasoning, published where the decision itself is published; the implementation timeline; the cadence of public reporting on implementation; and the mechanics by which the agreement is renewed or withdrawn. An agreement silent on any of these is incomplete, not merely informal.

**4.8** **Decision-to-implementation fidelity** — how faithfully what was decided is actually carried out, including the rate and reasoning of any override under 4.7 — is a tracked health metric for any live deployment, reported publicly on the same cadence as the deployment's other required reporting, and surfaced on the governance-health dashboard (Rules of Procedure 7).

## 5. A voice for organised business

**5.1** Local businesses and business organisations may establish a formal, non-voting advisory channel for budget and economic-development matters, distinct from Member deliberation and voting.

## 6. What this compact does not decide

**6.1** This compact commits to no permanent ceiling on which tiers of government may adopt Triarchal Democracy. Municipal, provincial or state, and national tiers are addressed explicitly because they are where real adoption is plausible first. Whether adoption extends above the national tier, or below the municipal, is left open, to be revisited once real evidence exists at the tiers this compact covers.

**6.2** Nothing in this compact overrides a jurisdiction's own law. Where this compact and local law conflict, this compact yields, and the deployment does not proceed until that conflict is resolved through legal means outside this document.

## 7. Subsidy and staffing reallocation

**7.1** As an explicit part of its adoption decision, or afterwards for a programme brought into scope under 7.1a — in either case separate from, and not required by, the ordinary discretionary budget delegation of Section 4 — an adopting government may elect to redirect an existing subsidy programme it directly controls into the dividend pool, subject to 7.1a. Only a government that actually administers the programme may redirect it: a municipal adoption cannot reach a provincial programme, a provincial adoption cannot reach a national one, and neither can reach the other's.

**7.1a** **Assistance programmes are outside the default scope.** A programme that rests on a disability determination, a need or means determination, or any other assessment of an individual's circumstances — disability support, income assistance, pensions and child benefits among them — is outside the scope of 7.1 unless the Members bring it in. No adoption decision reaches one by default, and no deployment plans, models or projects a figure on the assumption that one will be redirected. Members may bring such a programme into scope by ordinary proposal, under the same procedure as any other decision; an adopting government that controls it may then redirect it under 7.1 like any other subsidy. Everyone receiving it at the moment of transition is protected under 7.3.

**7.1b** The default reflects the posture of the project rather than a judgement about any one programme. Triarchal Democracy proposes a way for a community to decide things. It begins alongside the programmes that support people who need support, not in place of them, and at founding nothing in it draws on them. Whether one should ever be redirected is for the community to decide, in the open, when it judges the Foundation Dividend has grown enough to make the change worthwhile. Redirection works by closing a programme to new entrants — that is what releases the money — so a person who comes to qualify afterwards receives the Foundation Dividend instead of the programme's assessed entitlement. That is the trade any such proposal asks Members to weigh.

**7.2** **Release, not transfer.** Funds redirected under 7.1 enter the dividend pool only as the obligations protected under 7.3 fall away. Until then the adopting government continues to pay protected individuals itself, from the programme as it stands. The community neither holds, funds nor administers those payments, and they are not distributions under Founding Charter 5.5. Every sum that does enter the pool is recorded on the ledger as a distinct inflow — amount, date and source — under Treasury Code 1.1, and is distributed as any other pool money is.

**7.3** **Continuity.** Every individual receiving a programme redirected under 7.1 at the moment of transition continues to receive it from the adopting government, on the programme's own terms and indefinitely rather than on a timer. Neither Triarchal Framework nor the Triarc ever builds an eligibility test, for a redirected programme or for anything else. Everyone protected under this clause also receives the Foundation Dividend like any other Member, and contribution is recognised through the Civic Dividend. This clause applies in full to a programme brought into scope under 7.1a. The Triarc's governance may amend the administrative mechanics of continuity by ordinary proposal. Any amendment that would reduce, shorten or end what a person already protected under this clause receives is entrenched under Founding Charter 8.2.

**7.4** Implementing the protected track requires the adopting government to share, or itself continue to administer, the existing recipient list at the moment of transition — a real data-handling and privacy dependency, flagged here for the legal and technical review any specific adoption requires, and not resolved by this document.

**7.5** **Staffing budgets — vacant positions only.** Separately from any programme redirection under 7.1, and not requiring one, an adopting government may choose to redirect its own wage and staffing budgets. This is capped to vacant, unfilled roles and open budget lines: no currently filled government position may be redirected or eliminated as part of an adoption decision. A filled role enters scope only later, through ordinary attrition — the incumbent's own retirement or resignation — never as a direct effect of the adoption decision, and never through workforce adjustment, layoff, or the expiry of a term or casual contract the government chose not to renew.

**7.6** **Scope is not release.** A budget line entering scope under 7.5 becomes eligible for redirection; it is not thereby redirected. Each release is a decision the adopting government takes, and may decline to take, in the cycle it arises. No deployment may model staffing release as an automatic consequence of attrition, and no projection built on it may be presented as anything but an upper bound.
