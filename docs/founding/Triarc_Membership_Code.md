# The Triarc Membership Code

*A reference draft, offered as a Triarc-specific addition to Founding Charter Article I — not enacted law, and not applicable to any other deployment. The Triarc's own founding document, layered on top of the shared Charter rather than replacing it. If ratified, amendable by the Triarc's own ordinary proposal, per the Rules of Procedure.*

---

## 1. Why the Triarc diverges from the Charter's baseline

**1.1** Founding Charter 1.1 states the model's baseline: any person aged eighteen or older may become a Member by creating a verified account, declaring that age and affirming the charter, and the community may strengthen verification requirements by amendment as it grows. Every other deployment continues under that baseline exactly as written. This document applies to the Triarc alone and changes nothing about any other deployment's members or their standing.

**1.2** The Triarc differs in kind rather than in degree. It is the nonprofit itself: real governance of a real Canadian nonprofit corporation, whose directors are elected and whose resource decisions are approved through this deployment. Charter 1.1's baseline verification floor was written for an open community deployment. This document exercises the invitation contained in Charter 1.1 itself — to strengthen verification as the deployment grows — specifically for the Triarc.

## 2. The Triarc's membership floor

**2.1** In addition to Founding Charter 1.1's requirements, a Triarc Member's account must be tied to a unique, verified phone number. No phone number may be attached to more than one Triarc account. This floor is superseded automatically the moment the zero-knowledge identity architecture of Charter 1.5 and 7.1 exists for the Triarc.

**2.2** Deliberation, reading, following sectors, and logging contributions remain open to every registered Member immediately, exactly as the Charter provides. Clause 2.1 gates formal Membership, not participation short of it, and Rules of Procedure 2.4's lower-commitment public deliberation — open to visitors, not only Members — is untouched.

**2.3** A newly registered Triarc Member may vote directly on Ordinary and Treasury-allocation proposals immediately upon becoming a Member, exactly as any other Member, and may hold and receive delegations immediately.

**2.4** A newly registered Triarc Member's vote does not count toward a **Position decision** — installing or removing a Delegate, including in a director-election context under the Triarc's own bylaws — until they have held Membership in good standing for at least 30 days. This changes when a vote first becomes eligible on this proposal class. It never changes the weight of any vote once eligible, and it does not touch delegation, Ordinary, or Treasury-allocation voting.

**2.5** 30 days is a default, adjustable by the Triarc's own ordinary proposal like any other parameter in this document.

## 3. What this document does not do

**3.1** This document does not restrict who may become a Member based on their views, background, or any characteristic other than the verification floor in 2.1, applied identically to every applicant. Charter 1.4's protection against revoking membership for dissent or criticism applies with equal force to the decision to admit a Member in the first place. Clause 2.1 is a uniqueness check, never a viewpoint or identity screen.

**3.2** This document does not change anything about any other deployment. Their members joined, and continue to join, under Founding Charter 1.1 exactly as ratified.

**3.3** This document does not lower the bar the model commits to elsewhere. Charter 7.2's guarantee — a Member's legal identity is never required for participation and is never published by the community — is unaffected. A verified phone number establishes uniqueness to the platform, not public identity, and is never disclosed as part of a Member's public profile or the permanent record.

## 4. Legal status

**4.1** This document is a good-faith draft, not legal advice, and not yet reviewed by counsel. Canada's Not-for-profit Corporations Act requires a federally incorporated nonprofit's bylaws to state clear, administrable, non-arbitrary membership criteria before those criteria can seat directors or bind resource decisions. This document goes to counsel before the Triarc's bylaws rely on it, alongside the broader member-elects-directors mechanism it forms one piece of — the review the Adoption and Deployment Roadmap already schedules at Phase 1, step 3.

**Where does it fail?**

**4.2** **Schedule of defaults.** The one default in this code is **chosen** rather than derived. Thirty days of verified Membership before a vote counts toward a Position decision (2.4) is one contribution period: long enough that installing or removing a Delegate cannot be decided by accounts created once the vote was visible, and short enough that a Member who joined in good faith waits one cycle rather than a season. The limit of that is worth stating. A Position decision runs seven days of deliberation and five of voting under Rules of Procedure 1.3, so thirty days only stops accounts created reactively, inside or just before that window; accounts created a month or more ahead of a contested Position carry full weight, and nothing here detects intent. Ordinary and treasury proposals accept a Member's vote from the day they verify under 2.3, which is where treasury allocations are decided. It is adjustable by the Triarc's own ordinary proposal like any other parameter here.
