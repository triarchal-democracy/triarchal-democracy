# The Dispute Procedure Code

*A reference draft, offered to the community as a proposal — not enacted founding law. This code implements Article VI of the Founding Charter and the process requirements of the Member Bill of Rights and Code of Conduct. If ratified, amendable by ordinary proposal.*

---

## 1. What comes to a panel

**1.1** A panel hears contested contributions and attestations, alleged gaming of the Contribution Record, alleged Code of Conduct violations, contested findings from a custody reconciliation under Treasury Code 7.1, membership revocation, and any matter another document routes to a panel. Only a panel may order revocation. Routine quarterly reconciliations are conducted by their own smaller panel under Treasury Code 7.1 and come here only if a finding is contested.

**1.2** Panels interpret the documents. They do not make policy. A panel finding the rules themselves defective says so in its decision and refers the defect to the community as a proposed agenda item, rather than repairing the rule by verdict.

## 2. The pool

**2.1** Any Active Member may volunteer for the panel pool. Service accrues Stewardship Marks under the Contribution Code. The pool must hold at least 30 Members before any panel is drawn, so that a full eleven-seat draw still leaves real margin after the exclusions in 2.2. Below the minimum, disputes queue and the shortage is published.

**2.2** Excluded from any given panel: the parties; anyone holding or granting an active delegation to or from a party within the trailing 90 days; anyone who attested the disputed contribution; anyone with a pending dispute against a party; and anyone who served on a panel involving the same parties within the trailing 180 days.

## 3. Selection

**3.1** Eleven panelists are drawn from the eligible pool by verifiable public randomness. The selection method, seed, and result are published with the draw. The randomness is a round of a public randomness beacon, fixed before its value is known so that no one can choose it; the pool, the seed and the result are published, and anyone can re-run the draw from them.

**3.2** A drawn panelist may decline within 48 hours without stating reasons, and a replacement is drawn the same way. Once seated, a panelist serves through decision unless a disqualifying fact emerges, which any party may raise immediately.

## 4. Process and timeline

**4.1** A report states what happened, which provision it violates, and what evidence supports it. Reports are filed on the record.

**4.2** The accused receives the specifics and has 7 days to respond on the record. No decision issues before the response window closes, except where the accused waives it. Right IV of the Bill of Rights admits no exceptions.

**4.3** The panel deliberates on the record and decides by majority within 14 days of seating. The decision states findings, reasoning, the provision applied, and the sanction if any, beginning at the lowest adequate step of the Code of Conduct ladder. Dissents within the panel are recorded.

**4.4** The standard is the balance of evidence. The burden rests on the party asserting the violation. Doubt resolves toward the accused.

**4.5** Everything — report, response, evidence, decision, dissent — enters the permanent public record. Where evidence would expose a Member's outside identity (Right VI), the panel receives it privately and the record notes that it did, with the panel's characterisation standing in for the material itself. This is the record's only privacy carve-out.

## 5. Interim measures

**5.1** Moderators may act before a panel only for active, ongoing harm — removing doxxed content, freezing a suspected sybil account — per Code of Conduct 4.3: 72 hours maximum, lapsing automatically unless a panel reviews and extends. Every interim measure is logged when taken.

## 6. Appeal

**6.1** Per charter 6.3, a panel decision is overturned only by full community vote at the charter-amendment threshold: two-thirds majority, minimum participation of 20%. Any Member, not only a party, may bring the overturn proposal. There is no second panel.

**6.2** A revocation under Code of Conduct 3.5 does not take effect for 14 days after the decision. Any Member may file an overturn proposal within that window; if one is filed in time, the revocation remains paused until that proposal resolves, however long that takes. If no overturn proposal is filed within 14 days, the revocation takes effect automatically. Interim measures already restricting the Member for ongoing harm remain in force throughout.
## 7. Bad-faith process

**7.1** A panel finding that a report was brought in bad faith — knowingly false, retaliatory, or part of a pattern of harassment-by-process — may treat the report itself as a violation under Code of Conduct 2.1, in the same decision.

**Where does it fail?**

**7.2** **Schedule of defaults.** The one default in this code is **derived** rather than chosen, and it is the only derived default in this corpus. A pool of thirty Members (2.1) is the number at which an eleven-seat draw still leaves real margin after the exclusions of 2.2: a party to the dispute, everyone they delegate to, and everyone who delegates to them can all come out of the pool and a full panel can still be seated at random rather than from whoever is left. Treasury Code 5.1 uses the same figure for the dividend activation gate, but counts verified Members where this code counts Active Members who have volunteered for the pool. The two floors are therefore not reached at the same moment, and neither is evidence for the other. Treasury Code 5.1a records what that gap costs.
