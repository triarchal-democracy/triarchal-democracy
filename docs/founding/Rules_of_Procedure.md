# The Rules of Procedure

*A reference draft, offered to the community as a proposal — not enacted founding law. These rules implement Article III of the Founding Charter: the choreography of how proposals move, how votes are cast, and how delegation resolves. If ratified, amendable by ordinary proposal.*

---

## 1. Proposals

**1.1** Any Member may submit a proposal at any time. Per Right VIII, no person or body may prevent a proposal reaching deliberation because of what it argues.

**1.2** A proposal states, in order: what it changes (the exact text or action), why, what it costs if it touches the treasury, and how it can be reversed if it proves wrong. A proposal that cannot say how it would be reversed says why not.

**1.3** Proposal classes and their parameters:

| Class | Deliberation | Voting | Threshold | Minimum participation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ordinary | 30 days | 7 days | Simple majority | 10% |
| Treasury allocation | 30 days | 7 days | Simple majority | 10% |
| Position decision | 7 days | 5 days | Three-fifths | 20% |
| Charter amendment | 45 days | 5 days | Two-thirds | 20% |
| Entrenched amendment | 60 days | 7 days | Three-quarters | 40% |

Spending from the treasury reserve is not an ordinary Treasury allocation: Treasury Code 2.2 sets it at the Charter amendment threshold — two-thirds with 20% minimum participation — while keeping the Treasury allocation timeline.

**1.3a** An Ordinary or Treasury allocation proposal with a genuine external time constraint — a grant application closing, a contractual deadline, a real window that would close before 1.3's default timeline could run — may petition to run on an **accelerated timeline** of 7 days' deliberation and 5 days' voting. The petition succeeds on the same signature standard as 1.6's critical-classification petition, at least 10% of Active Members stating the specific deadline, but is filed before deliberation opens rather than before voting. No other class may be accelerated. A proposal may be both critical and time-constrained; the two petitions are independent, and a proposal may carry either, both, or neither.

**1.3b** The minimum participation column above states percentages only. Every one of them carries the ten-Member floor of charter 3.5: the requirement is the greater of the percentage and ten Members, and never more Members than the community has. At the thirty Members that activate distribution, that floor binds on four of the five classes — it is the operative requirement for ordinary and treasury proposals at ten per cent, and for Position decisions and charter amendments at twenty, where twenty per cent of thirty is six. Only the entrenched class escapes it, because forty per cent of thirty is twelve. The percentage overtakes the floor at different sizes for different classes: once the community passes fifty Members for the twenty-per-cent classes, and not until it passes one hundred for the ten-per-cent ones. Above one hundred Members the floor never binds on any class again.

**1.4** All windows are minimums measured in full days from public posting. Nothing may be deliberated, voted, and recorded in the same day.

**1.5** A **Position decision** recognises or removes a Delegate under Charter 4.3. Contribution Code 8.6's nomination-fit challenge reuses this proposal class directly.

**1.5a** A Delegate whose support falls into Charter 4.8's **recall band** is put to a **confirmation vote**: a Position decision under 1.3, opened automatically rather than proposed by a Member, asking whether they keep the Position. It runs on the Position decision class's own timeline and threshold, and the incumbent votes in it as any Member would. Falling into the band removes no one by itself: the Position is held until the confirmation vote resolves, and Charter 4.9's separate automatic-removal path is untouched.

**1.5b** A **concentration review** (Charter 3.6) is a Position decision under 1.3 asking whether delegations to a named Member in a named sector continue. It opens automatically on the first tick on which that Member holds the review share, and not while a review of them in that sector is open or within the review interval after a ratified one. The Member votes in it as any Member would, and delegations to them count in it as in any vote. Shares are counted from live delegations against the ordinary renewal cycle, an entire delegation counting in every sector. A failed review ends sector-scoped delegations to them in that sector and every entire delegation to them; each ended delegation is revoked on any open vote as 3.3 provides, and its delegator may delegate elsewhere at once. Opening a review removes no one's delegation by itself.

**1.6** A proposal is **critical** if it falls in the Charter amendment, Entrenched amendment, or Position decision class, or if it is a Treasury allocation proposal exceeding 5% of the treasury's total balance at time of submission. Any Member may petition to classify another proposal as critical; the petition succeeds if it gathers signatures from at least 10% of Active Members before voting opens. A critical decision requires delegations renewed within the shorter cycle set by Charter 3.3 (default: 90 days). A delegation last renewed outside that window still carries an ordinary vote, but not a critical one.

## 2. Deliberation

**2.1** Deliberation is open to every Member, asynchronous by design, and on the record. Arguments accrue Deliberation Marks only when other Members attest they moved the decision.

**2.2** The proposer may accept amendments at any time until the vote freeze, 24 hours before voting opens, after which the text is fixed.

**2.3** A Member whose amendment is declined may fork the proposal. Forks run on the same clock as the original. If conflicting proposals both pass, the one with the larger share of votes cast prevails, and the conflict and its resolution are recorded.

**2.4** Deliberation is open to the public, not only to Members. A visitor may read every proposal and, at low commitment — a first comment, a question — post under a lower-visibility identity rather than a persistent handle. That post earns no Deliberation Mark and carries no weight in the record's attribution. Contribution Marks, attestation, and any binding act — voting, delegating, submitting a proposal — always require the Member's persistent identity per Charter 7.2.
## 3. Voting

**3.1** Voting is open for the full window. Votes are public and permanent, per Article VII. The Triarc does not have secret ballots; it has the Bill of Rights against retaliation instead (Right III). Accountability of the vote is what makes delegation trustworthy.

**3.2** Every Member may vote directly on any proposal. A direct vote always overrides any delegation for that proposal.

**3.3** Delegation resolves at the moment voting opens: the platform records a snapshot of standing delegations, resolves each chain to its terminal Member, and publishes the resolution with the vote. Delegations *granted* during the voting window apply to the *next* vote. Every *revocation* takes effect instantly — per Charter 3.3 and Right III, whether full or scoped to a single sector. The delegated weight is withdrawn from the delegate immediately, and if the delegate has already cast their ballot, that weight stops counting toward it at the same moment. The delegate's own vote is untouched. The un-delegated Member may then vote directly until the window closes.

**3.4** Minimum participation counts Members voting directly plus Members whose vote arrived through delegation. A vote failing to meet it fails without prejudice: it may be resubmitted once after 14 days, and if it fails twice, the record notes that the community declined to take it up.
## 4. Scheduling

**4.1** Proposals open on a weekly cycle (default: Mondays). Emergency proposals — those addressing active harm to the community — may open immediately by petition of 10% of Active Members, with all other windows unchanged.

**4.2** No more than 5 proposals enter voting in any week (default). When more qualify, they queue in submission order.

**4.3** The platform may surface proposals, delegations, and opportunities relevant to a Member, and may never manufacture urgency to do so. No countdowns, streaks, or guilt-based prompts.

**4.4** Where the platform ranks or surfaces proposals, opportunities, or Stewards and Delegates for a Member, the ranking blends exactly two public signals under a disclosed, amendable weighting — the two weighted equally by default: sectors the Member has declared they follow (Contribution Code 2.4), and overlap between the Member's own sector-tagged Contribution Record and the item's sector tags. No other signal — no browsing, clicks, or private behaviour — ever feeds it. The full, unranked, chronological list of every proposal remains equally available at all times.
## 5. The record

**5.1** Each concluded proposal produces a permanent record entry: final text, full deliberation, the vote — every ballot and every delegation resolution — the outcome, and, for any Member who requests it, their recorded dissent, per Right X.

**5.2** The record is append-only. Errors are corrected by correction entries that reference what they correct; nothing is edited in place. This guarantee is enforced today by application-level append-only convention and staged toward the ledger's own cryptographic chaining as Charter 7.1 sets out.

**5.3** Any Member may formally attach a **minority report** — a written, attributed dissenting case — to a major proposal: a Charter amendment, an Entrenched amendment, a Position decision, or a Treasury allocation proposal exceeding the critical share of 1.6. A minority report is preserved alongside the proposal on the permanent record, in full, whether the proposal passes or fails. Filing is opt-in: nothing is generated automatically, and a proposal with no filed minority report carries none.

## 6. Co-Delegate arrangements

**6.1** When Charter 4.10 finds two candidates for the same Position closely tied in delegated support, the Position decision installs both jointly as co-Delegates. The proposal states plainly that it is installing a joint arrangement, not a single Delegate, before the vote opens.

**6.2** Each co-Delegate holds unilaterally: proposing, deliberating, and voting as a Member exactly as before; representing the Position in routine, non-binding communication; and logging Craft, Stewardship, and Deliberation contributions under the Position as any Delegate would.

**6.3** The following require both co-Delegates acting together: nominating or revoking a Supporting Nominee under Contribution Code 8; committing the Position to a public position on a pending proposal in its own institutional capacity, as distinct from either co-Delegate's personal vote; and any treasury allocation request the Position initiates.

**6.4** Either co-Delegate acting alone may respond to active, time-sensitive harm within the Position's sector when the other is unreachable, subject to the same 72-hour interim-measure limit and mandatory review the Code of Conduct sets for moderators. Every such action is logged publicly the moment it is taken.

**6.5** A deadlock between co-Delegates on a joint-authority matter under 6.3 is resolved by referring the specific question to a community vote at the Ordinary threshold.

**6.6** One external spokesperson for the Position is designated at a time — by mutual agreement between the co-Delegates, reaffirmed at each renewal cycle, or, absent agreement, by whichever co-Delegate holds the larger share of delegated support as of that cycle. Spokesperson status confers no additional authority.

**6.7** Compensation for the Position splits evenly between the co-Delegates by default, adjustable only by mutual written agreement between them, logged on the public record.

**6.8** Either co-Delegate may be removed individually — by Position decision, or by falling below the thresholds of Charter 4.7 through 4.9 — without affecting the other's standing. If a removal leaves one co-Delegate holding the Position alone, they continue as a sole Delegate under Charter 4.3, subject to the same thresholds as any other Delegate. The Position does not automatically revert to requiring a second holder.

## 7. Governance-health reporting

**7.1** The community record includes a public, continuously updated **governance-health dashboard**, computed from the public record itself. It tracks, at minimum: delegation concentration (the largest Delegate's share, the top-5 and top-10 shares, and the percentage of Members holding no active delegation); contribution distribution (concentration, domain diversity, and attestation concentration); institutional concentration (how concentrated Position, Supporting Nominee, moderator, and Dispute Panel service are among a small set of Members); pluralism indicators (the number of independent active leadership networks, and proposal-authorship concentration); resilience (how many Members can perform each critical function, and how dependent the community is on the founder or any specific administrator); and, for any live Delegated-Authority Mechanism deployment, delegated-authority-exercised and implementation/override rates under the checklist of Government Adoption Compact 4.7 and the fidelity metric of 4.8.

**7.2** The dashboard's methodology — what each indicator measures and how it is computed — is community-amendable by ordinary proposal, the same rule Contribution Code 2.1 applies to domain weighting.

**7.3** The dashboard is informational only. Nothing on it automatically restricts, blocks, or triggers any action against any Member, Delegate, or proposal.

**Where does it fail?**

**7.4** **Schedule of defaults.** Every default in these rules is **chosen** rather than derived. The class timings of 1.3 mostly scale with what is at stake: thirty days to deliberate an ordinary proposal and forty-five or sixty for an amendment, on the reasoning that the cost of a slow good decision is lower than the cost of a fast bad one, and that a charter amended in a fortnight is a charter nobody had time to read. The Position decision class is the deliberate exception — seven days, the shortest in the table, despite being critical under 1.6 — because a Position decision is about a person and a sector's office, and a month of public deliberation over whether someone keeps their Position leaves the Position in limbo and the person in a month-long referendum on themselves. The voting windows beside them are shorter than the deliberation that precedes them because by the time a vote opens the argument has been had: seven days for an ordinary or treasury proposal, five for a Position decision or a charter amendment, and seven again for an entrenched amendment, where the longest deliberation is paired with the longer window rather than the shorter one. The thresholds rise with what a decision can undo: a simple majority for the ordinary, three fifths to install or remove a person, two thirds to amend the charter, three quarters for the entrenched provisions. The participation percentages rise with them for the same reason, and every one of them carries the ten-Member floor of 1.3b. All of these are chosen, not derived. The twenty-four-hour vote freeze (2.2) is one sleep — enough that a late surge has to survive the night. No more than five proposals may enter voting in a week (4.2) because a community that cannot read its ballot is not deliberating. The five per cent of treasury balance that makes an allocation critical (1.6) is set low deliberately: the question it asks is not “is this a lot of money” but “is this large enough that it should move on current trust rather than standing trust” — critical classification does not buy anyone more time to think, it requires delegations renewed inside the shorter cycle of charter 3.3, so a stale delegation carries an ordinary vote but not this one. Monday openings (4.1) are a convention, not a requirement, and exist so Members can learn one rhythm rather than track five. The ranking weights of 4.4 — following and contribution, weighted equally by default — are a deliberate refusal to decide, in advance and for everyone, which of the two says more about a Member; they are disclosed precisely so the community can argue about them.
