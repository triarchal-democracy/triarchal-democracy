# The Founding Charter

*The governing constitution of the Triarc — the nonprofit organisation behind Triarchal Democracy, and the first deployment of the model it proposes.*

---

**How to read this document.** Numbered clauses are the rules. They bind. The indented notes between articles explain why a rule is built the way it is; they carry no force, and nothing turns on them. Where a clause states a number as a *default*, the community can change it by ordinary proposal without amending this charter.

---

## Preamble

The Triarc governs itself by the model it proposes. Verified membership, a public contribution record, liquid delegation, a transparent treasury, earned return — every mechanism described here is both how the Triarc operates and a live test of whether the model works. The name is the structure: three arcs — governance, contribution, return — closing one circuit.

This charter is a starting position, not a finished constitution. Nearly everything in it can be changed by the community it governs, through the process it defines. The numbers are defaults, not doctrine.

We want the argument more than the agreement. Where does it fail?

---

## Article I — Membership

**1.1** Any person aged eighteen or older may become a Member of the Triarc by creating a verified account, declaring that they have reached that age, and affirming this charter. Verification at founding means a unique account in good standing, and the community may strengthen it by amendment. The declaration a Member makes about age is a declaration, not a check of age; when it becomes known that a Member is under eighteen, their account is closed and its personal data deleted, their public Contribution Record remains, and they may join again at eighteen.

**1.2** Each Member holds exactly one vote on every binding decision. Membership, votes, and standing cannot be purchased, sold, inherited, or transferred. No mechanism adopted under this charter may create a second vote for any person.

**1.3** A Member may leave at any time. Their Contribution Record is preserved as part of the public history. Their standing, and any delegations they hold or have granted, lapse immediately.

**1.4** Membership may be revoked only by a Dispute Panel (Article VI), and only for conduct that materially damages the community. It may never be revoked for the content of a Member's positions, their dissent, or their criticism of the community itself.

**1.5** Verification may check a prospective Member's government-issued identification or financial records for one purpose: to confirm they are a real, unique person not already a Member. It may serve no other purpose. The record retains the pass/fail result and nothing else; underlying documents are not retained beyond what law requires and never enter the public record. A Member may ask what was checked and how. No Member, and no part of the Triarc's own governance, may ever see what a check surfaced about another Member. Verification follows a staged cryptographic architecture:

- **Stage 1 (where the deployed platform stands today).** Conventional secure authentication. The pass/fail guarantee above is enforced by access control and policy, not yet by cryptography.
- **Stage 2.** Cryptographic integrity proofs over the verification record itself.
- **Stage 3.** Privacy-preserving identity mechanisms that reduce what the platform can see, even internally.
- **Stage 4.** Full zero-knowledge identity architecture: the check happens once, produces a cryptographic proof of uniqueness and eligibility, and discards the underlying data entirely.

This charter commits to reaching Stage 4. It does not claim the platform has already arrived there.

## Article II — The Contribution Record

**2.1** Every Member has a Contribution Record: a public, permanent, append-only ledger of their verified contributions to the community.

**2.2** At founding, contributions accrue in four domains:

- **Deliberation** — substantive participation in proposals: arguments, evidence, and critique that other Members attest moved a decision. Criticism that changes a document counts equally with authorship of it.
- **Craft** — research, writing, code, design, translation, and documentation contributed to the project.
- **Stewardship** — moderation, onboarding of new Members, facilitation of deliberation, maintenance of community infrastructure, and in-person service to the community carried out through a host organisation the community has recognised.
- **Attestation** — peer recognition of contribution that resists automatic measurement. Attestations are public and attributed; an attestation is itself a small contribution by the person making it.

**2.3** Contribution must fit the time people actually have, not the meetings they can attend. Asynchronous participation carries full weight. No contribution domain may be structured so that only Members with abundant free time can accrue in it.

**2.4** Domain weightings are set by the community and reviewed on a fixed cycle (default: quarterly). They are changed by ordinary proposal.

**2.4a** Every community-set default in these documents is accounted for — at the clause that defines it, or in that document's schedule of defaults — as **derived** from something measurable or **chosen**, and with what it assumes. A number nobody can account for is a number nobody will re-examine, and the defaults in a governance document are where unexamined assumptions do the most damage: they look like findings. Where a default is chosen, saying so is not a weakness in the document but the thing that makes it amendable on evidence later.

**2.5** The Contribution Record determines a Member's civic return (Article V) and civic standing (Article IV). It never alters the weight of any binding vote.

---

**2.6** **Schedule of defaults.** No default in this charter is derived from evidence; each is a judgement, and each is adjustable by ordinary proposal unless its clause says otherwise. Some are better accounted for than others, and this schedule says which. Quarterly review of domain weightings (2.4) is short enough to correct a bad weighting inside a year and long enough that weights are not a standing argument; any period under a year would satisfy the first half of that, and the quarter is chosen to sit alongside the other quarterly cycles rather than add a sixth rhythm. Delegation expiring after a year, and after ninety days on critical decisions (3.3), is set so a delegation survives ordinary inattention but not indifference. The ten per cent renewal share and twenty per cent review share (3.6) are judgements about when delegated support has stopped being one Member's trusted reach and become a standing block: ten per cent is the figure Article IX's health criterion already uses for the largest holder, twenty is double it, and the ten-supporter floor is the same floor every participation requirement carries, so that a sector of three delegations is never called concentrated. Minimum participation (3.5) is accounted for at 3.5a. The five per cent installation threshold (4.3) confers eligibility only — a Position decision still has to vote — which is why it carries no absolute companion as 4.7 does; five per cent is set low because the threshold opens a question rather than settling one. The two-supporter condition (4.7) is accounted for at 4.8a. The magnitudes beneath it are the thinnest-evidenced numbers in this article and are stated as such. The sustaining floor of three per cent is three fifths of the installation threshold, so a Position stops being held automatically — and a confirmation vote opens under 4.8 — once support falls below three fifths of what would newly open the question; automatic loss comes lower still, below the recall trigger of one and a half per cent (4.9), which is half the floor again. Both ratios describe the threshold and not the Delegate: 4.3's five per cent is eligibility to be installed, never the support any particular Delegate holds, so a Delegate installed with far more than five per cent can shed most of it and still sit above the floor with no review. That gap is real and these clauses do not close it; annual expiry under 3.3 is what eventually does. Both magnitudes are chosen, neither is derived, and a deployment with real delegation data should expect to move them. The fourteen-day grace period (4.9) is two weekends, on the reasoning that rebuilding support is work people do when they are not at work. The two per cent tie margin (4.10) is proportional deliberately and does not begin to bind until a sector holds roughly fifty delegations — below that only an exact tie is a tie. The accrual cap (5.3) is accounted for at 5.3a. Fourteen days for a panel to decide (6.2) is one working fortnight, long enough to read a record and exchange views without the dispute becoming the panel's second job; the forty-five and sixty days of amendment deliberation (8.1) are accounted for in Rules of Procedure 7.4, which gives the reasoning the window lengths actually rest on. None of the three is derived, and the distance between fourteen days and sixty is a judgement about how a panel's work differs from an amendment's, not a calibration of either. An Active Member is one with a verified contribution in the trailing ninety days (9.1) — a quarter, chosen so that “active” tracks what a Member is doing rather than what they once did. A Member away for a full season does lapse, and becomes Active again on their next verified contribution; the status is a description, not a sanction, and nothing in these documents treats losing it as a penalty. The Phase I and Phase II criteria (9.2, 9.3) are a judgement about what a community running without its founder looks like, not a measurement of one, because no such measurement exists — and, apart from 9.3's limit on the founder's share of completed proposals, what they measure is activity rather than independence. The Member counts, completed lifecycles and delegation movements they require could in principle all be produced by one operator running many verified accounts, which is the single thing they exist to rule out. They should be among the first defaults revisited against real evidence. They are not, however, the most consequential numbers here: both are amendable under 9.6, and 9.5 lets the community end founding authority at any time without either being met. The most consequential chosen number in these documents is the thirty-Member activation gate of Treasury Code 5.1, because charter 5.4 makes what it triggers permanent — the one default whose consequences a later community cannot amend away. Treasury Code 5.1a accounts for it, and says plainly that nothing in the evidence fixes it at thirty. The ten per cent delegation concentration ceiling of 9.3 is chosen too, and it sits awkwardly with a deployment carrying few sectors: the Triarc's own list defines three Positions (Triarc Operations Code 3.1), so meeting a ten per cent ceiling requires delegated trust to spread well beyond its Delegates rather than concentrating on them. That may be the right pressure or the wrong one. It is not a measurement either.

## Article III — Governance

**3.1** Binding decisions are made by proposal. Every proposal moves through four public stages:

1. **Submission** — any Member may submit. A proposal states what it changes and why.
2. **Deliberation** — open discussion for a fixed window, set by proposal class (Rules of Procedure 1.3). Amendments may be offered and accepted by the proposer, or forked as competing proposals.
3. **Voting** — a fixed window, likewise set by proposal class. Members vote directly or through standing delegation.
4. **Record** — the outcome, the vote, every delegation exercised, and the deliberation itself enter the permanent public record.

**3.2** On any proposal, every binding vote is one Member, one vote. Contribution earns standing and visibility; only delegation, freely granted, aggregates votes.

**3.3** A Member may delegate their vote to another Member, by sector or entirely. Delegation is public, specific, and revocable at any instant. It expires on a fixed cycle (default: annual) unless actively renewed. Critical decisions, classified under the Rules of Procedure, carry a shorter renewal requirement (default: 90 days).

**3.4** Delegation chains resolve to a single voting Member; circular delegations are void. Every Member can see, at any moment, exactly where their vote sits.

**3.5** Ordinary proposals pass by simple majority of votes cast, subject to minimum participation — **the greater of 10% of Members and ten Members** — voting directly or by delegation. Both the percentage and the absolute floor are community-set and adjustable by ordinary proposal. **Every minimum participation requirement in these documents carries the same ten-Member floor, whatever its percentage**, and none may ever require more participants than the community has Members. Charter amendments require the thresholds in Article VIII.

**3.5a** The absolute floor exists because a percentage alone is not a quorum at founding scale. At the thirty Members that activate distribution under Treasury Code 5.1, ten per cent is three voters, and a simple majority of three is two. Two people should not be able to carry a proposal that binds thirty, least of all one that resets a treasury parameter. The floor binds only while a community is small: above one hundred Members the percentage is the greater of the two and the floor never applies again.

**3.6** Delegation may aggregate votes, but it must not gather them without limit, and no vote is ever capped or discounted: a delegation counts in full or has lapsed. A Member who holds at least ten supporters in a sector, and 10% or more of that sector's active delegation (default: 10%), is a **concentrated holder** there, and every delegation to them is judged against the shorter renewal cycle of 3.3 on every vote, critical or not. A concentrated holder at 20% or more (default: 20%) is put to a **concentration review**: a confirmation vote, opened automatically rather than proposed by a Member, asking whether delegations to them in that sector continue. Ratifying keeps them, and no further review opens for 180 days. Failing to reach the Position decision threshold ends every live delegation to them that applies in that sector, returns each delegator's vote to the delegator, and bars any new delegation to them for 90 days. Rules of Procedure 1.5b specifies the mechanics.

## Article IV — Standing, Stewards, and Delegates

**4.1** Civic standing is the visible, earned reputation a Contribution Record establishes. It confers eligibility and visibility, never vote weight.

**4.2** A Member with a sustained Contribution Record in a sector (Contribution Code 2) who receives delegated trust from other Members on that sector is recognised as a **Civic Steward** for it. A Member may hold Steward recognition in more than one sector, each independent of the others. Stewardship carries no authority beyond the delegations that constitute it.

**4.3** Where the treasury can compensate sustained civic work, a Steward holding delegated support above the installation threshold within a sector (default: 5% of the sector's active delegated support) becomes eligible to be recognised as a **Delegate** for that sector, compensated from the treasury by public allocation. A Member may hold Delegate recognition in more than one sector, each decided independently. Recognising a Delegate is decided by a Position decision (Rules of Procedure). The community may remove a recognised Delegate by Position decision at any time, independent of their delegation total. There is no term and no immunity period. Clauses 4.7 through 4.9 govern loss of a Position between votes.

**4.4** The community record includes a public directory of every Civic Steward and Delegate, organised by sector, showing their Contribution Record and who currently delegates to them. Recognition that cannot be seen is not accountable; visibility is not optional.

**4.5** No founding member, including the author of this charter, holds any standing not earned under Article II or any authority not granted under Article III — subject only to the temporary provisions of Article IX.

**4.6** A Delegate holding a defined Position may nominate Supporting Nominees to assist in that Position's work, under the mechanics the Contribution Code defines. A nomination confers a recognised role only, and never any share of the nominating Delegate's vote weight or delegated authority. Any Member may challenge a specific nomination; the nature of the challenge determines whether it is heard by a Dispute Panel or by community vote, per the Contribution Code and Rules of Procedure.

**4.7** A **sustaining floor** — **the greater of** a community-set percentage of the sector's ongoing active delegation, set below the installation threshold of 4.3 (default: 3%), **and two supporters** — is the level at or above which a Delegate's Position remains automatically held, with no review and no vote required.

**4.8** A **recall band** sits between the sustaining floor of 4.7 and the automatic-removal trigger. Support below the sustaining floor but at or above the trigger (default: 1.5% of the sector's active delegation) neither keeps the Position automatically nor removes it: it opens a confirmation vote at the Position decision threshold, run under Rules of Procedure 1.5a.

**4.8a** The two-supporter condition in 4.7 is what makes this band reachable. Expressed as a percentage alone, a single supporter is 1/S of a sector holding S active delegations — which is at or above a 3% floor for every sector smaller than thirty-four delegations. A Delegate holding exactly one supporter would hold their Position automatically and indefinitely, and the confirmation vote this clause provides could never be triggered, in precisely the communities where one supporter is least evidence of support. Annual expiry under 3.3 would still eventually lapse the delegation; this clause is what lets the community act before then.

**4.9** Support dropping below the automatic-removal trigger of 4.8 opens a grace period (default: 14 days) during which the Delegate may rebuild support above the trigger. If support has not recovered when the grace period ends, the Position is lost automatically, without a vote.

**4.10** Where two candidates for the same Position are tied within a margin of the sector's active delegated support (default: 2%), the Position is held **jointly**. The Rules of Procedure specify: which authorities each co-Delegate holds unilaterally; which require both; any unilateral emergency authority; how a deadlock is resolved; who serves as external spokesperson; how compensation splits; and how either co-Delegate may be removed individually — by Position decision or under 4.7 through 4.9 — without affecting the other's standing.

## Article V — Treasury and Return

**5.1** The treasury is funded by donations, grants, and any revenue the community votes to pursue. Every inflow and outflow is recorded on a public ledger at the moment it occurs. An audit is not something the community requests; it is something the ledger performs continuously.

**5.2** Treasury allocations are made only by proposal under Article III. No individual, including any founder, may unilaterally move community funds.

**5.3** The **Civic Dividend** is the community's first live return mechanism. On a fixed cycle (default: monthly), a community-set share of the treasury is distributed to Members in proportion to Contribution Records for that period, subject to a per-Member accrual cap (default: no Member may receive more than **five times an equal share** of the new money a cycle commits to distribution, counting both treasury-funded tiers together, and no other payment — an equal share being that new money, without amounts carried forward under Treasury Code 4.3, divided by the number of verified Members entitled to a dividend under Treasury Code 9.1). The cap exists because a system that rewards engagement must not be captured by those with the most hours to spend, and expressing it as a multiple rather than a fixed sum means it tightens automatically as the community grows.

**5.3a** The multiple is **chosen**, and it encodes an expected participation rate as well as a ceiling. Because the cap counts both treasury-funded tiers together, every Member already holds their Foundation share against their own ceiling, so the Civic headroom left to any one Member is five equal shares less that Foundation share. Full distribution of a cycle therefore needs one Member earning for every (5 − s) ÷ (1 − s) Members, where s is the Foundation Dividend's share of the pool under Treasury Code 5.4: at the default half, one in nine — a little over eleven per cent of the community active in that cycle. Below that the cap withholds money no contributor is eligible to receive, and what it withholds is not lost: it joins the next cycle's Foundation Dividend under Treasury Code 4.3, so it reaches every Member rather than piling up. The one-in-five that reads naturally off “five times” is the figure for a Foundation share of zero, which 5.4 does not permit; both numbers move if either default moves, which is why the relation is stated here and not only its result. A flat percentage was rejected for this because it is wrong at both ends — binding so hard at founding scale that most of a cycle strands, and so loosely at scale that a handful of Members could absorb a community's whole dividend. Below ten Members the Civic headroom exceeds the whole Civic pool and the cap binds on nobody, which is correct — a cap against taking many times your neighbours' share has nothing to do when there are fewer neighbours than the multiple — and also unreachable, because Treasury Code 5.1 does not start distribution until thirty.

**5.4** The **Foundation Dividend** — the universal tier, unconditional between Members once live — activates once the community has at least 30 verified Members and every Delegate Position it has defined has been filled by ratified Position decision (Treasury Code 5.1). Before that, neither tier distributes. After it, the tier is permanent: never re-gated, never suspended, never re-tested, whatever later happens to membership or to any Position. The amount is recalculated every cycle from what the treasury can sustain (Treasury Code Section 5).

**5.5** Distributions never diminish because a Member's circumstances improved. A **distribution** is a payment from the community treasury: the Civic Dividend and the Foundation Dividend. A payment reaching a Member from outside the treasury, which the community neither funds nor administers, is not a distribution under this charter.

**5.6** Where a government formally delegates budget authority to a Triarc deployment, that delegation is governed by the Government Adoption Compact, not by this article. This article governs the Triarc's own treasury, raised and spent under its own governance; delegated public budgets remain a distinct category of funds even when Members' votes decide both.

## Article VI — Disputes

**6.1** Contested contributions, contested attestations, alleged gaming of the Contribution Record, and alleged conduct violations go to a **Dispute Panel**: eleven Members drawn at random from a pool of volunteers, excluding parties to the dispute and their active delegates or delegators. The pool must hold at least 30 Members before any panel is drawn (Dispute Procedure Code 2.1).

**6.2** Selection is verifiably random and public. Panels deliberate on the record and decide by majority within a fixed window (default: 14 days). Decisions, with reasoning, enter the permanent record.

**6.3** A panel decision may be overturned only by a full community vote at the charter-amendment threshold.

## Article VII — Transparency

**7.1** All governance records — proposals, deliberations, votes, delegations, revocations, contribution entries, attestations, treasury movements, and panel decisions — are public and permanent. Append-only integrity follows the same staged architecture as verification (1.5):

- **Stage 1 (where the deployed platform stands today).** Append-only by application convention: enforced by access control and audit, not yet by cryptography.
- **Stage 2.** Cryptographic integrity proofs anchoring each entry as it is written.
- **Stage 3.** A fully public, cryptographically chained ledger — the destination: no party, including the Triarc itself, can alter or delete an entry once it is recorded, and that guarantee no longer rests on policy or trust alone.

This article commits to reaching Stage 3. It does not claim the platform has already arrived there. This does not extend to custody of funds themselves (Treasury Code 6.4); it extends only to the record of what happened.

**7.2** Members participate under persistent identities. A Member's legal identity is never required for participation and is never published by the community. What must be public is what a Member *does* within the community, not who they are outside it.

**7.3** Nothing that binds the community on the record may happen off it. Private conversation is private; private governance is void.

## Article VIII — Amendment

**8.1** Any Member may propose amendment to this charter. Amendments follow Article III with a longer deliberation window (default: 45 days; 60 days for the entrenched provisions of 8.2) and pass at a two-thirds majority of votes cast, with minimum participation of 20% of Members.

**8.2** Seven provisions are entrenched, amendable only by three-quarters majority with minimum participation of 40%:

- One Member, one vote on every binding decision (1.2, 3.2)
- The complete public permanence of governance records (Article VII)
- The prohibition on purchasing, inheriting, or transferring standing or votes (1.2, 2.5)
- The Member Bill of Rights, in full — from the moment the community ratifies it
- The Foundation Dividend formula, which no proposal may set aside (Treasury Code 5.7)
- Continuity for every person already receiving a programme redirected under the Government Adoption Compact: nothing they receive may be reduced, shortened or ended (Compact 7.3)
- The entrenchment of this section: the list of entrenched provisions in 8.2, and the majority and participation it requires, cannot be shortened or lowered by any lesser majority (8.2)

**8.3** Entrenchment exists because these properties are what make every other property trustworthy. A community that can easily vote away its own transparency, its own rights, or the support it promised the people who relied on it, has already lost them. 8.2 protects itself for the same reason: a list of protections that a lesser majority could shorten would protect nothing.

## Article IX — The Founding Period

**9.1** Founding authority ends in stages, and every stage ends on evidence rather than on a calendar. Each transition is triggered by measured criteria computed automatically and continuously from the public record. The founder plays no role in declaring a criterion met and no role in delaying a transition once the criteria are met. An **Active Member** is a Member with at least one verified contribution in the trailing 90 days. All numbers in this article are defaults, amendable by the community under 9.6 and not by the founder.

**9.2 Phase I — Genesis.** The founder may set initial parameters, appoint interim moderators, and correct defects in community infrastructure without proposal. Every such action is recorded publicly and is reversible by ordinary proposal in later phases. Phase I closes automatically when all of the **Foundation Criteria** hold simultaneously:

- 150 verified Members, of whom at least 50 are Active
- 50 proposals have completed the full lifecycle — submission, deliberation, vote, record
- Minimum participation has been met in a majority of votes held over the trailing 90 days
- At least 40 delegations are active, and at least three delegations have been revoked or lapsed and renewed
- Contributions have been verified in all four domains of Article II

**9.3 Phase II — Shared governance.** All ordinary proposals bind by community vote under Article III. The founder retains one power: a suspensive veto over charter amendments and treasury movements above a community-set threshold. A suspensive veto must be exercised publicly with written reasoning, delays the measure for at most 30 days, and is overridden by a two-thirds majority. Phase II closes automatically when all of the **Sustainability Criteria** have held continuously across a trailing 180-day window:

- 500 verified Members, of whom at least 150 are Active, with net Active Membership non-declining across the window
- Governance runs at cadence: an average of at least 6 proposals per month completing the full lifecycle, with minimum participation met in at least 75% of votes
- Delegation is healthy: renewals functioning, and no Member holds more than 10% of all delegated votes
- The treasury is self-sustaining: at least 8 consecutive scheduled Civic Dividend distributions executed on time — measured across their own consecutive span, which necessarily runs longer than this article's 180-day window — with reserves covering at least 12 months of operating costs at the current rate
- The dispute system has been exercised: at least 5 panels convened and resolved within their windows
- Leadership has depth: at least 8 Civic Stewards hold sustained Contribution Records, spanning at least 3 sectors
- The community runs without its founder: proposals the founder authored make up less than a quarter of the proposals completing the full lifecycle across the window

**9.4 Phase III — Full handover.** When the Sustainability Criteria close Phase II, founding authority terminates entirely and automatically. From that moment the founder is a Member, holding exactly the standing their Contribution Record has earned and exactly one vote.

**9.5 The ejection clause.** At any time, in any phase, the community may terminate founding authority — in whole or in any part — by a two-thirds majority of votes cast with minimum participation of 20%, following the ordinary process of Article III. The founder's suspensive veto does not apply to such a proposal, and the founder does not vote on it.

**9.6** Amendments to this article follow Article VIII, with two restrictions: the founder's veto does not apply to them, and no amendment may weaken or remove 9.5 while any founding authority exists.

**9.7** The engagement basis is deliberate. The founder of Bitcoin did not leave on a schedule; he left when the network demonstrably ran without him. A handover keyed to a calendar can transfer a constitution to a community that does not yet exist. A handover keyed to evidence transfers it to one that has already proven it can carry it.

**9.8** This article self-repeals at full handover. Its text remains in the record as history.

## Closing

This charter will be wrong in places. Some of its defaults will prove naive, some of its mechanisms will be gamed in ways it did not anticipate, and some of its assumptions will not survive contact with real people. That is what it is for. Every failure discovered here is a failure no city has to discover later.

The record of what this community does under this charter — every decision, every delegation, every dollar — is the evidence Triarchal Democracy will one day carry into the world.

**Where does it fail?**
