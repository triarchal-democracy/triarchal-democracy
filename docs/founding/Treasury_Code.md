# The Treasury Code

*A reference draft, offered to the community as a proposal — not enacted founding law. This code implements Article V of the Founding Charter. If ratified, amendable by ordinary proposal. Ratifying, amending, or replacing it is among the Triarc's first legislative acts.*

---

## 1. What enters the treasury

**1.1** The treasury is funded by donations, grants, and revenue the community votes to pursue. Every inflow is recorded on the public ledger at the moment it occurs: amount, date, and source.

**1.2** Money must never buy influence, and the ledger must make that verifiable. Donations above a disclosure threshold — **the lower of $1,000 per source per year and 2% of the treasury's total inflows over the trailing twelve months** — must be publicly attributed.

**1.2a** The threshold is the lower of the two because a fixed sum *alone* discloses least exactly where a single donor matters most. Were it a flat thousand dollars, a community that had taken in three thousand over the year would let a nine-hundred-and-ninety-nine-dollar gift — a third of everything it had raised — go unnamed, while at five million of inflows that same figure is noise and names nearly every donor regardless. The share is what corrects both ends: two per cent of three thousand is sixty dollars, so at that scale the $999 gift is named and so is far less than it; two per cent of five million is a hundred thousand, and there the flat thousand does all the work. Taking the lower of the two means a small treasury always discloses more, not less. Read the measure exactly — it is inflows over the trailing twelve months, not the balance, so a treasury that has spent what it raised still discloses against what it raised. The cost is real and is accepted deliberately: early donors have less anonymity than later ones, which is the stage at which anonymity would buy the most. Anonymous or pseudonymous donations above the threshold are declined or returned. Below it, sources may remain anonymous in public view and are logged internally against the threshold.

**1.3** No donation, however large, purchases standing, Contribution Marks, delegation, or any governance consideration. A donor who is a Member holds exactly one vote. Accepting a donation conditioned on any governance outcome is prohibited; discovering such a condition after acceptance voids it, and the funds are returned.

**1.4** Contributions may carry purpose restrictions — to operations, to the reserve, to a specific ratified project. Restrictions are honoured and logged on the ledger with the inflow, and restricted contributions bypass the standing allocation of Section 2, flowing only to their stated purpose. Institutional contributors, including the Triarc itself, contribute to operations and infrastructure and never to the dividend pool: the dividends are the community's money, generated and governed by the community alone. A restriction directs where money goes and never purchases any say in anything else. Clause 1.3 applies to institutions exactly as to individuals.
## 2. How money is divided

**2.1** Net inflows divide by standing allocation, adjustable quarterly by ordinary proposal. Founding defaults:

| Allocation | Share | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Operations | 40% | Infrastructure, tooling, services the community votes to fund |
| Dividend pool | 40% | Both tiers: the Foundation Dividend share first (5.4), the Civic Dividend from what remains |
| Reserve | 20% | Untouched except by explicit proposal; target of 12 months' operating costs |

**2.2** Until the reserve reaches its target, its share may not be reduced. Spending from the reserve requires the charter-amendment threshold: two-thirds with minimum participation of 20%.

## 3. How money leaves

**3.1** Every outflow requires either a ratified proposal under the Rules of Procedure or a line in a ratified recurring budget. There is no third path. No individual — founder, steward, moderator, keyholder — may move funds unilaterally.

**3.2** Recurring operating budgets are proposed and ratified quarterly, itemised, including any Delegate compensation under charter 4.3. Spending against a ratified budget is executed by the custodians and logged per transaction.

**3.3** During Phase II of the founding period, treasury movements above the veto threshold — a community-set share of the treasury's balance when the proposal opens, **default: 10%** — are subject to the founder's suspensive veto under charter 9.3: public reasoning, 30 days maximum, two-thirds override.

**3.3a** The threshold is a share rather than a fixed sum because a fixed sum moves founding authority the wrong way as the community grows. Set at five thousand dollars, the veto would never engage at all while the treasury held less than that — the stage at which a single bad allocation does the most damage — and would reach essentially every allocation once the treasury passed a few hundred thousand, handing the founder the most reach at the point charter Article IX intends them to have the least. A share holds the founder's remit at the same relative size throughout: the veto reaches the same fraction of the treasury at every scale, which is what keeps Article IX's arc pointing the right way. In absolute terms the threshold rises as the balance rises, and that is intended — a larger community moves larger sums for ordinary reasons, and a share rises with them where a fixed sum would not. One gap is left open and is recorded here rather than left to be found: 3.3 tests each movement, so an allocation can be split into tranches that each fall below the threshold. The veto is a delay and not a block, and splitting costs the proposer separate votes on the public record, which makes this a weak evasion rather than a free one. It is still an evasion, and nothing in this code closes it.

## 4. The Civic Dividend

**4.1** Monthly, the dividend pool distributes to Members in proportion to the period's claimed Contribution Marks under the Contribution Code, subject to the per-Member cap of charter 5.3 — five times an equal share, measured against the new money committed to that cycle (amounts carried forward under 4.3 excluded), both treasury-funded tiers together and no other payment, not against the Civic Dividend alone — and drawn from whatever remains in the dividend pool after that cycle's Foundation Dividend share (5.4) is set aside first.

**4.2** Distribution is automatic on a published schedule. A distribution may be paused only by ratified proposal, never by administrative discretion.

**4.3** Undistributed residue from the Civic Dividend, whether from the cap or from rounding, is carried forward into the next cycle's Foundation Dividend and divided equally among every verified Member. It never reverts to operations, it is never paid in the cycle that produced it, and it never raises the next cycle's cap, which is measured on that cycle's new money alone. Carrying it to the Foundation Dividend is what keeps it from piling up: whenever fewer than one Member in nine contributes, contributors are already at the cap, and residue that could only go to them would never be paid at all. Contributions that lapse unclaimed under Contribution Code 1.4–1.6 generate no residue at all: under 4.1's proportional division they never enter the denominator.

## 5. The Foundation Dividend

**5.1** The universal tier **activates** once both of these hold, and stays active permanently once they have: the deployment has at least **30 verified Members**, and every Delegate Position it has defined has been filled by ratified Position decision. Before activation the formula in 5.3 through 5.5 computes zero and neither tier distributes. After it, the mechanism is never absent, never re-gated, never suspended, and never re-tested, whatever later happens to membership or to any Position.

**5.1a** The membership floor of 30 is a chosen threshold, not a derived one, and it is the most consequential chosen number in these documents: charter 5.4 makes the activation it triggers permanent, so this is the one default whose consequences a later community cannot amend away. Nothing in the evidence fixes it at thirty. It is the number at which an equal share of a plausible founding pool is large enough to be worth paying, and the community large enough that paying it is not a transfer among friends — a judgement, not a measurement. The same figure appears as the panel-pool minimum in Dispute Procedure Code 2.1, where it genuinely is derived, but it counts a different population there: Active Members who have volunteered for the pool, not verified Members. A deployment reaches this gate well before it can draw a panel, and in that interval disputes queue under 2.1 while the dividend runs. That gap is a cost of starting the return early, stated here deliberately; it is not two numbers agreeing for a reason. The Position condition is policy: a return paid out before anyone holds accountable office over the treasury funding it is a return nobody can be answerable for. Both conditions are one-way by design.

**5.2** Once activated under 5.1, every verified Member receives the same per-Member amount each distribution cycle, on the same cadence as the Civic Dividend, unconditional between Members and regardless of contribution level.

**5.3** The amount is dynamic, recalculated from the dividend pool's actual health every cycle, and never fixed or promised in advance. The formula in 5.4 and 5.5 can never distribute more than the pool contains that cycle, and computes zero for every cycle before 5.1's activation.

**5.4** Each cycle, a community-set **share of the dividend pool's new money** — default: **one half**, adjustable by ordinary proposal — together with any residue carried forward under 4.3, is the first-priority claim on that cycle's pool, and is divided equally among every verified Member. The Civic Dividend's earned distribution under Section 4 draws on what remains. Because the Foundation Dividend is a share of the pool rather than a fixed per-Member amount, it always fits within the pool and no cycle requires it to be scaled down.

**5.4a** The share is a proportion rather than a fixed amount for the same reason charter 5.3's cap is a multiple of the equal share rather than a flat percentage: a fixed amount is wrong in both directions at once. When the pool is small a fixed floor consumes all of it and the Civic Dividend pays nothing — removing the return that sustains contribution at exactly the stage a deployment most needs it. When the pool is large the same fixed amount dwindles to a token, and the universal tier stops being universal in any sense that matters. A proportion scales with the pool at every size. The default of one half says plainly what the two tiers are for: half of the pool is returned because a person is a Member, and half because of what they contributed. What that is half *of* should be read exactly: the dividend pool is two fifths of net inflows under 2.1, so the Foundation tier is a fifth of what the treasury takes in, and inflows are donations, grants and whatever revenue the community votes to pursue — not a measure of what the community produces.

**5.5** The Foundation Dividend takes no in-cycle top-up from Civic Dividend residue. Residue joins the next cycle's Foundation Dividend under 4.3 instead.

**5.6** Funding sources are pooled: the dividend pool draws from the community treasury's standing 40% dividend-pool allocation (Section 2) and, for deployments operating under the Government Adoption Compact's Delegated-Authority Mechanism, delegated public-budget funds the deployment agreement explicitly designates for this purpose. Each dollar's source — donation or redirected public funds — remains tagged on the public ledger, per 1.1's amount, date and source standard.

**5.7** No proposal, at any threshold, may set the Foundation Dividend to an amount other than what 5.3 through 5.5 compute for that cycle. The formula is not a policy input subject to override by vote. Only its parameters — the pool share of 5.4 — are, and those follow the ordinary amendment process like any other default.

## 6. Custody

**6.1** Funds are held under multi-party control: no fewer than three custodians, any transaction requiring a majority of them. Custodians are elected by ordinary proposal, serve staggered renewable terms (default: 6 months), and may be removed at any time by ordinary proposal.

**6.2** Custodians execute what ratified proposals direct. A custodian who declines to execute a ratified allocation is removed, and the refusal enters the record.

**6.3** During Phase I, one custodian seat may be filled by a representative the Triarc appoints institutionally, rather than a named individual acting on personal discretion. That seat may never constitute a majority of custodians, and it is replaced by an elected custodian under 6.1 on the same timeline as any other Phase I transition.

**6.4** Custody is conventional, not cryptographic: treasury funds are held in fiat, through regulated financial institutions, never in cryptocurrency. The governance record, including the treasury's own transaction history, is committed to the staged cryptographic-integrity path Charter 7.1 sets out. The funds themselves are never part of that path.
## 7. Audit

**7.1** The ledger is the audit: continuous, public, and complete, per charter 5.1 and Article VII. Each quarter a randomly selected three-Member panel, using Dispute Procedure Code selection mechanics, reconciles the ledger against external statements and publishes its reconciliation. Finding nothing is a finding, and is published too.

## 8. Delegated public budgets

**8.1** Funds under delegated public-budget authority (Government Adoption Compact, the Delegated-Authority Mechanism) are held, tracked, and reported separately from the treasury described in Sections 1–7, and are never blended into the operations or reserve lines of Section 2. One route in exists: funds a deployment agreement explicitly designates for the dividend pool may be pooled into the Foundation Dividend under 5.6, provenance-tracked as 5.6 requires. Absent that explicit designation, delegated public-budget funds stay separate from the dividend pool as well.

**8.2** Deciding how already-collected public money is spent is real economic influence, even though it changes no tax rate and claims no private property.

**8.3** Delegated public-budget funds carry whatever government-grade reporting and audit standard the delegating jurisdiction requires, in addition to — never instead of — the continuous public ledger this code requires.

## 9. Who receives the dividend, and custody of invested funds

**9.1** Dividend entitlement follows membership. Every verified Member is entitled to the dividend, and no separate age condition applies here: Founding Charter 1.1 admits only people who have declared that they are eighteen or older.

**9.2** For the purposes of Founding Charter 5.3, an equal share is the dividend pool divided by the number of verified Members.

**9.3** Governance rights are not conditioned on dividend entitlement, and dividend entitlement is not conditioned on governance participation.

**9.4** Funds the community holds other than in its immediate operating account — including the reserve required by Section 2 and any invested assets — are held by an arm's-length regulated financial institution appointed as custodian under a written custody agreement, and not by the community itself. The requirements of Section 6 bind the custodian's mandate as they bind the treasury: conventional fiat holdings, regulated institutions, multi-party authorisation, no cryptocurrency. The custody agreement and the identity of the custodian are public.

**9.5** The investment mandate for any custodied assets is conservative and diversified — capital preservation and modest real growth, never speculation. The specific mandate (permitted asset classes, rebalancing rules, a named benchmark) is set by ordinary proposal, reviewed annually, and published. The custodian executes the mandate; the community does not trade.

**9.6** Custodied assets are audited on the same terms as the treasury under Section 7 — continuous public ledger, quarterly randomly-selected reconciliation panel — with results reported separately and reconciled against the custodian's own statements.

**Where does it fail?**

**9.7** **Schedule of defaults.** Every default in this code is **chosen** rather than derived. 1.2a, 3.3a and 5.4a account for the *form* of three thresholds — why the disclosure threshold is the lower of two measures, why the founder's veto threshold is a share rather than a sum, why the Foundation Dividend is a proportion rather than a fixed amount — and 5.4a accounts for its magnitude as well. The magnitudes in 1.2 and 3.3 are not accounted for anywhere: a thousand dollars, two per cent of trailing inflows and ten per cent of the balance are chosen figures with nothing behind them but a judgement about the right order of size, and a deployment with real donation and allocation data should expect to move all three. The activation gate of 5.1 is accounted for at 5.1a, which says the same of thirty and explains why that one matters most. The 40/40/20 split of 2.1 is a judgement about proportion, not a finding: operations and the dividend take equal shares on the reasoning that a community whose return is a residual will not sustain contribution, and a community that cannot pay its bills will not have a return to divide; the fifth held back is what lets the other four fifths be spent without gambling. Nothing fixes the ratio at 40/40/20 rather than 45/35/20. The reserve target of twelve months' operating costs, set in 2.1's table and protected by 2.2, is four budget cycles — 3.2 ratifies operating budgets quarterly — long enough to survive a bad year without being so large that the community is saving rather than doing. Three custodians (6.1) is the smallest number that bars any one person from moving money while still surviving the loss of one: two would also bar a single actor, but two leaves no margin for illness, departure, or a refusal to execute under 6.2. What three does not do is stop two custodians acting together, because 6.1 requires a majority and two is a majority of three. The control against that pair is the ledger, which shows the movement to every Member, not the custody rule — and raising the number raises both the safety and the chance of never assembling a quorum to pay an invoice.
