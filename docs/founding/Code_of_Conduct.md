# The Code of Conduct

*Proposed under the Founding Charter of the Triarc — a founding proposal, not yet law. This code gives content to charter clause 1.4 — what "conduct that materially damages the community" means — and binds sanctions to the graduated process the Member Bill of Rights requires. If ratified, amendable by ordinary proposal.*

---

## 1. What the Triarc expects

**1.1** Argue in good faith. Attack positions, never people.

**1.2** Distinguish what you know from what you believe. Cite what can be cited. Say "I think" when you think.

**1.3** Represent your contributions truthfully.

**1.4** One person, one account, one voice.

## 2. Violations

The following materially damage the community, in ascending order of gravity:

**2.1** **Bad-faith disruption** — sustained derailing of deliberation, flooding, or persistent personal attacks after warning.

**2.2** **Contribution fraud** — claiming work not done, inflating work done, or colluding in attestation rings to farm the Contribution Record.

**2.3** **Vote and delegation interference** — buying, selling, coercing, or threatening any Member's vote or delegation, or retaliating for either.

**2.4** **Sybil violation** — operating or knowingly assisting multiple member accounts.

**2.5** **Infrastructure sabotage** — deliberate damage to the platform, records, or treasury mechanisms.

**2.6** **Exposure** — publishing a Member's outside identity against their will, or credibly threatening to.

Criticism of the Triarc, advocacy of any position, and unpopular argument appear nowhere on this list and never will. Right I of the Bill of Rights is not subject to this code.

## 3. Graduated sanctions

Panels must begin at the lowest step adequate to the harm.

**3.1** **Public notice** — a panel finding entered in the record, with no further consequence.

**3.2** **Accrual suspension** — contribution accrual paused for a defined period (default: 30 days). Participation, voting, and voice continue unimpaired.

**3.3** **Participation suspension** — posting and proposal rights paused for a defined period (default: 30 days, maximum 90). The Member's vote is unaffected: under Right II, only revocation itself touches the vote.

**3.4** **Contribution voidance** — for fraud under 2.2, the fraudulent entries are voided, and dividends paid on them are owed back to the treasury before accrual resumes.

**3.5** **Revocation** — membership ends. Reserved for violations 2.4 through 2.6, or persistent repetition of lesser violations after sanctions at every lower step. Only a Dispute Panel may revoke. The decision stands unless the community overturns it under charter 6.3, and Dispute Procedure Code 6.2 holds the revocation for 14 days so that proposal can be filed.

## 4. Process

**4.1** Any Member may report a violation. Reports go to a Dispute Panel under Article VI of the charter: eleven randomly selected volunteers, parties and their delegation relationships excluded, deciding on the record within the defined window.

**4.2** The accused Member receives the specifics of the allegation and responds on the record before any decision. Right IV admits no exceptions.

**4.3** **Moderators** are Members recognised to a moderation Position under Contribution Code 8, installed and removed by Position decision exactly as any other Delegate Position. During the founding period the founder may appoint interim moderators instead (Charter 9.2); that power lapses with Phase I, and from Phase II onward the role exists only by community vote. A moderator may take emergency interim measures — removing doxxed content, freezing a suspected sybil account — lasting no more than 72 hours, after which a panel must review or the measure lapses automatically. Moderation carries no authority over any proposal, vote, or delegation.

**4.4** Every finding, sanction, and reasoning enters the permanent public record. Sanctions expire; the record does not.

**4.5** **Schedule of defaults.** Both defaults in this code are **chosen** rather than derived. Thirty days of accrual
suspension (3.2) is one full contribution period, so a sanction costs a cycle rather than an arbitrary fraction of one.
Participation suspension (3.3) carries the same thirty-day default with a ninety-day maximum, the ceiling set so that no
sanction short of the Dispute Procedure's own remedies can remove someone from community life for a season. Neither default
touches the vote, which is not suspendable under any provision of these documents.

## 5. The spirit clause

No code anticipates everything. A panel confronting conduct this code did not foresee should ask one question — does this conduct damage the community's capacity to govern itself honestly? — and answer it with the lightest hand the answer permits.
