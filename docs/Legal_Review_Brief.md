## 1. What we are asking for, in one paragraph

We propose to incorporate a federal not-for-profit that will pay recurring amounts to the people who
use its software. We think we have found a structure that keeps those people outside the
corporation's membership, so that the Act's restriction on distributing property to members has
nothing to attach to, while preserving a real accountability chain from those people to the board.
**We want to know whether that structure holds, and what it costs us if it does not.** We would
rather be told early than build further on something that cannot carry weight.

Charitable registration is **not** being sought. That decision is made and is not open.

---

## 2. The proposed structure — two layers

**Layer 1 — the deployment. Everyone.**
Participants hold full governance rights under our own constitutional documents: one vote on every
binding decision, revocable delegation, an earned contribution record, and both tiers of a periodic
payment (an unconditional per-person floor, and an earned amount proportional to a verified
contribution record). **They are not members of the corporation.** Their relationship to it is
contractual — participants, in the sense that a guaranteed-income pilot has participants.

**Layer 2 — the corporation. A small elected class.**
The CNCA membership is the holders of three elected operational roles ("Delegates"), chosen by
Layer 1 through a vote that already exists and runs. Those members elect and remove the directors,
by ordinary vote. During the first phase, founder-named initial directors sit alongside, as the Act
requires at filing.

**The rule that closes the loop: a corporate member takes no payment from the pool while serving.**
Accepting a Delegate role means entering the membership and suspending payment entitlement for its
duration. Delegates are compensated for the role instead, which our founding documents framed as
payment for sustained work before any of this arose.

**The intended consequence:** no member of the corporation receives a distribution. Participants are
paid as participants. Delegates are paid for services. Directors are elected by members, as the Act
requires. Members are elected by participants, so the accountability chain is one step long and
entirely public.

## 3. Facts you may need

1. **The corporation.** Proposed federal NFP, not yet incorporated, no members today. Its stated
   activity is governance research: it studies whether a governance model works by operating on that
   model itself, in public, and publishing results on a fixed quarterly cadence. It also develops and
   maintains the open-source software the research runs on, under AGPL-3.0.
2. **The two payment tiers differ in shape**, and we suspect that matters. The earned tier is
   proportional to a verified contribution record and capped per person — it resembles compensation
   for services or research participation. The unconditional tier is paid to every participant
   regardless of contribution — it resembles a distribution far more closely. We would value your
   view on whether they should be analysed separately.
3. **Nothing is being paid to anyone.** A gate is built and live in the software: no payment of
   either tier occurs until the deployment reaches 30 verified participants with all three Delegate
   roles filled. We can incorporate, file, bank, take grants and run the research long before this
   becomes live.
4. **The vote that elects Delegates is unusual** — standing, revocable at any instant, and
   transferable between participants by subject area. Under the proposed structure it operates only
   in Layer 1. See Q5 on why we think that removes it from your scope.
5. **Participant identity** is established by a verified unique phone number, never a legal name. Our
   constitutional documents guarantee that a participant's legal identity is never required for
   participation and never published.
6. **Every participant must be eighteen or older, and the dividend has no separate age condition.** **Decided 20 October 2026**: the minimum age to join is eighteen, a fixed number rather than the age of majority where each person lives. Each participant declares at joining that they are at least eighteen, and the app records that declaration and its date. No birthdate or other age data is collected or stored. An account found to belong to someone younger is closed and its personal data deleted. An earlier design admitted participants from thirteen and withheld the dividend until the age of majority; it was removed before any member joined. In places where the age of majority is above eighteen (nineteen in some Canadian provinces, twenty-one in a few countries), an eighteen-year-old would be entitled to a payment before that age. See Q16.

## 4. The questions

Ranked. **Q2 and Q3 are the ones that could force a redesign**, and we would rather pay for those
first and the rest later than have all sixteen answered slowly.

**Q1 — Is the membership class permissible as constituted?**
Membership would consist of whoever currently holds three elected operational roles, and would begin
and end automatically with the role. Can a CNCA membership be defined by reference to an elected
office in that way? Does membership need to be capable of existing independently of the role, and if
so what is the minimum that must be true?

**Q2 — Does the separation actually avoid the distribution restriction?**
This is the question we most need answered. Assuming participants are not members, is a recurring
payment to them clear of the restriction on distributing property to members?

- **(a)** If entitlement accrues to someone *before* they become a member, and is paid afterwards, is
  that still property distributed to a member? This is the sharpest edge we have found in our own
  proposal.
- **(b)** Does the unconditional tier need a different characterisation from the earned tier, or does
  the non-membership of recipients settle both at once?

**Q3 — Is Delegate compensation distinguishable from a distribution?**
Delegates are members and are paid for their role from the same treasury. Is reasonable compensation
for services distinguishable from a distribution to a member — and does the answer change where a
Delegate is also a director?

**Q4 — What must the participant relationship look like?**
Does it need to be a documented contract to keep participants clearly outside the membership? What
must it say, and what must it avoid saying? Is there language that would accidentally create
membership?

- **(a)** Our documents let unpaid, in-person service count toward the earned payment when a host organisation the community has recognised — a library, a school, a food bank — confirms it (Contribution Code 3.5). Does crediting physical work in this way move the relationship toward employment, create liability for an injury, or trigger any insurance or workers' compensation obligation, and if so for whom: the corporation or the host?

**Q5 — Does the Act reach Layer 1 at all?**
Our proposal assumes the Act governs the corporation and not the deployment's internal governance. Is
that assumption safe? If any part of Layer 1 is reachable — because it determines who the members
are — we would like to know which part.

**Q6 — The filing sequence.**
The Act requires initial directors named at filing, and a first bylaw is typically made by directors
and confirmed by members at the next meeting. Our bylaw would define who the members are. Who are the
members at the moment such a bylaw takes effect, and who confirms it? We would like the exact order
of operations from filing to a member-confirmed bylaw.

**Q7 — Where must the keystone rule live?**
The rule that closes Q2 — *a corporate member takes no dividend while serving* — currently appears in
our movement's documents (Federation Compact Article VIII), which are not the corporation's bylaw and
are explicitly not part of our own constitution. We assume it has to be in the bylaw, and probably in
the articles, to be worth anything to you. Confirm, and tell us the wording you want.

**Q8 — Does our use of the word "dividend" create a problem?**
Our public documents describe a recurring payment to participants as a *dividend*, offered alongside
an invitation to join. We collect no money from participants and offer no return on any contribution
of capital, so we believe there is no securities or investment-scheme issue. We would like that
confirmed rather than assumed, and we would like to know whether the vocabulary alone creates
exposure or a regulator-attention problem worth renaming to avoid.

**Q9 — Advocacy as a stated activity.**
Our documents permit the organisation to argue publicly for legislative measures, including ones affecting income support, and to publish evidence from its own operation in support of them. We are not
seeking charitable status, so we assume the political-activity limits that would apply to a charity do
not apply to us. Confirm, and tell us whether this needs to appear in the stated purposes at filing or
is better left out of them.

**Q10 — Can the corporation receive redirected public funds as an ordinary inflow?**
Our documents let a government that adopts redirect an ordinary subsidy programme it controls. Programmes resting on a disability, need or means determination are outside the mechanism by default (Government Adoption Compact 7.1a), but Members may bring one into scope by ordinary proposal; the same question applies to them if they do, together with one more: is anything owed to a person who would have qualified for such a programme after the transition, and who instead receives only the Foundation Dividend? The
government continues to pay everyone already on the programme, on the programme's own terms; only funds
released as those obligations end enter our treasury, recorded as a distinct inflow and distributed like
any other pool money (Government Adoption Compact 7.2, 7.3). What we need to know: does
receiving public money on those terms raise any issue for the corporation — reporting obligations,
restrictions on use, or its status — and is there anything the redirection agreement itself must say?

**Q11 — If the structure fails, which fallback?**
We are prepared to move. Two we have considered:

- **An unpaid oversight class.** Five participants elected by Layer 1, receiving no compensation and
  no payment, whose only function is electing and removing directors. Removes Q3 entirely at the cost
  of another elected body.
- **A co-operative, or a second entity holding the payment function.** Co-ops distribute surplus to
  members natively rather than by exception. We have deliberately not decided between NFP and co-op,
  and would rather hear that the NFP cannot carry this than discover it later.


**Q12 — Does the custody arrangement need a trustee, or registration?**
We propose to appoint an arm's-length regulated financial institution as custodian of the reserve
and any invested assets, under a public written agreement, with the investment mandate set by a vote
of the organisation's participants and executed by the custodian. Does that arrangement require
anything further — a trustee, a registered manager, a prospectus exemption?

**Q13 — Whose duty runs to the funds, and can a vote set the mandate?**
The mandate is set by ordinary proposal — a vote of participants who are not the corporation's
members. Where does fiduciary duty sit, and is a popular vote a permissible way to set an investment
mandate over pooled funds?

**Q14 — What changes if participants may opt to defer and invest?**
We have deliberately **not** adopted this, on the assumption that a participant electing to leave
money in a pooled vehicle expecting a return is closer to an investment contract than to custody, and
would trigger registration. Confirm or correct that assumption. If it is wrong we would adopt it; if
it is right, we would like to know whether any exemption realistically reaches an organisation of
this size.

**Q15 — How are the payments taxed, and how do other programmes treat them?**
Two parts. First, the corporation: does paying participants who are not members keep it within the
income-tax exemption for non-profit organisations, which requires that no income be available for
the personal benefit of a member? Second, the people paid: are the unconditional tier and the earned
tier taxable in their hands, and are they treated differently from each other — and will income-tested
programmes such as the Guaranteed Income Supplement or provincial disability and income assistance
count either tier as income? Our pages currently say only that this is not settled. We would like to
be able to say what a recipient actually keeps.

**Q16 — Is a self-declared minimum age of eighteen, with no birthdate stored, adequate given it gates a cash payment?**
Decided 20 October 2026 (see Fact 6): at joining, each participant declares that they are at least eighteen, and the app records only that declaration and its date; no birthdate or other age data is collected or stored anywhere. Payment of either tier turns on this single, unverified, self-reported fact. We would like to know: does this expose the corporation to liability if a declaration is false and a person under eighteen receives payment; is there a lighter-weight step short of collecting a birthdate (a jurisdiction-appropriate disclaimer alongside the declaration, for instance) that would reduce that exposure; is a fixed eighteen defensible where the age of majority is higher; and does the answer change with the amount paid or the jurisdiction?

## 5. Decided, and not open

Stating these so the engagement does not spend time reopening them:

- **No charitable registration.**
- **No cryptocurrency, no tokens, no on-chain anything.** The treasury is conventional fiat custody
  through regulated institutions.
- **Federal incorporation in Canada**, unless you tell us that is wrong for what we are doing.
- **The software stays AGPL-3.0**, and any deployment can fork and self-host it.
- **The two-layer separation is an incorporation pattern only.** We have already rejected promoting it
  into the governance model, and do not want it recommended back to us as a governance improvement.

## 6. What we want out of the engagement

In order:

1. **A first conversation scoped to Q2 and Q3 only** — an hour, not a retainer. If the answer is "this
   does not hold", we want to know before anything else is built on it.
2. **If it holds:** the wording Q7 needs, then the filing sequence at Q6.
3. **Then the rest**, and incorporation.

We would rather have a short, blunt answer to the two questions that matter than a thorough memo on all sixteen.
